LIKELY NOVEL

CA86: CA(86; 3, 40, 3)

Independent prior-art investigation • Research cutoff: November 14, 2026

Executive conclusion

The evidence supports submitting CAN(3,40,3) ≤ 86 as a new best-known upper bound. This investigation found no earlier public array, verified theorem substitution, or inspected dataset entry establishing 86 or fewer rows for 40 ternary columns. The strongest verified exact-target predecessor has 87 rows. [S01] [S02]

The closest disconfirming lead is unusually close: Bai’s original October 30 deposit contains CA(86;3,39,3) alongside CA(87;3,40,3). The former lacks one column; no inspected extension theorem supplies it without additional search. Independent exhaustive verification of the latter found every row essential: deleting any row loses at least 25 required interactions. [S02]

This conclusion rests on source files and parameter calculations beyond the maintained table: original deposits, historical bounds, larger-alphabet and higher-strength records, recursive constructions, supplementary arrays, independent repositories, recent unmerged submissions, and citation searches. The result is a strong research assessment, not a proof that no undiscovered disclosure exists.

5. Recursive and alternate representations

A consequential product-construction error

Cohen–Colbourn–Ling Theorem 3 requires two strength-three base arrays. Its product bound is CAN(3,kℓ,v) ≤ CAN(3,k,v) + CAN(3,ℓ,v) + CAN(2,ℓ,v)·DCAN(2,k,v). Grömping’s report §7.8.3 incorrectly labels the second base ingredient strength two. The tempting arithmetic 27+14+9·5=86 therefore uses an inadmissible 14-row ingredient and is not prior art for the target. The primary theorem and proof resolve the ambiguity. [S12] [S13]

Covering schemes

A ternary scheme with M rows develops to a covering array with 3M rows, so ≤28 scheme rows on ≥40 columns would be a concrete conflict. The 2024/2025 recursive paper was obtained as public author full text; the initial publisher access gap was resolved. Let Cₜ(k)=CSN(t,k,ℤ₃). Its relevant recurrences are: [S11]

C₃(k+1) ≤ C₃(k)+6C₃(2k) ≤ C₃(k)+2C₂(k)C₃(kℓ) ≤ C₃(k)+C₃(ℓ)+(C₂(k)−1)(C₂(ℓ)−1)−1C₃(k+ℓ−2) ≤ C₃(k)+C₃(ℓ)−1

An exhaustive finite closure of these recurrences, projection and the paper’s strongest ternary seeds (9 rows at width 4, 11 at width 6, 14 at width 8) reaches at most 18 columns within 28 rows, even with unrealistically cheap strength-two ingredients: 3 at width 3, 4 at width 4 and 5 at every larger width. Using lower ingredient costs enlarges the attainable parameter set; failure in this relaxation rules out this particular recursive route. The script and results are included.

These strength-two ingredient costs use the paper’s all-differences covering-scheme convention. CCL’s nonzero-only difference covering arrays use a different convention; their minima are not interchangeable. Later 2026 scheme papers give no qualifying seed or smaller ingredient that closes the gap. [S11] [S12] [S26] [S27]

Orthogonal, higher-strength and mixed arrays

A direct ternary strength-four source with ≥40 columns and ≤86 rows is impossible by a simple slicing argument: fix two columns, leaving nine slices that each cover all pairs on ≥38 columns. Each slice needs ≥9 rows. At exactly 9 it would be an OA(9;2,38,3), but orthogonality of the two contrasts per column gives at most four columns. Every slice therefore needs ≥10 rows, so N≥90. Higher strength also implies strength four.

A ternary strength-three orthogonal array on 40 columns obeys the Rao bound N≥1+2·40+4·39=237. A linear-code construction with dual distance ≥4 is an OA and has the same obstruction. For mixed arrays, at least 40 columns must each have ≥3 symbols; binary columns cannot count toward the target. Accessible mixed-level results supplied no qualifying witness or demonstrated reduction. Relevant full-text gaps are listed in Section 9.

8. Novelty assessment and recommendation

Level | Assessment | Reason

1. Numerical bound | LIKELY NOVEL | No prior public result found establishing CAN(3,40,3) ≤ 86. The strongest verified exact predecessor is 87; nearest same-row predecessor has only 39 columns.

2. Explicit construction | No prior qualifying witness found | No inspected explicit array with ≤86 rows and ≥40 ternary columns. Candidate file absent, so this audit does not claim a direct isomorphism comparison or independent candidate validation.

3. Search method | Broad ingredients established; exact refinement unassessed | Tabu search and postoptimization/row deletion have extensive prior art. Bai’s own paper describes deletion followed by tabu repair. Specific block-swap design requires actual algorithm/code and comparisons.

Nurmela’s tabu work dates to 2004; whole-row postoptimization appears in Nayeri–Colbourn–Konjevod’s 2009 proceedings paper. Bai’s October 2026 methods already use row deletion and tabu repair, sometimes after a column extension. These facts qualify an algorithmic novelty claim without undermining a new numerical witness. [S37] [S36] [S04]

Recommended claim

“We exhibit a CA(86;3,40,3), improving the best previously located public upper bound for CAN(3,40,3) from 87 to 86. The construction was obtained by postoptimizing Bai XunDi’s 87-row extension of the Shokri–Moura lineage.”

Use “new best-known upper bound,” not an exact value for CAN. The investigation supports that claim as of November 14, 2026, subject to the matrix satisfying the supplied verifier claims.

Recommended submission destination and evidence

First destination: the maintained ca-tools record table. Its current policy accepts explicit arrays/certificates through the submissions directory and distinguishes exact VERIFIED entries from screening. It accepts AI-assisted searches with appropriate disclosure and credit. No submission was made in this investigation. [S35]

A reviewable submission should include: (1) the canonical 86×40 text matrix and its SHA-256; (2) an exhaustive verifier and output for all 266,760 requirements, ideally retaining the independent reviewer implementation; (3) authorship and discovery timestamp; (4) exact parent-array hash, Bai dataset DOI and Shokri–Moura citation; (5) a reproducible description of deletion, repair and block swaps, including seeds/software versions when available; and (6) this prior-art audit or a concise equivalent explaining the 86×39 near miss and why the former exact-target bound is 87.

A versioned public data deposit with a short computational note is the appropriate durable scholarly companion. A standalone journal claim about the search technique should wait until the block-swapping mechanism and comparative evidence support a separate contribution. The numerical record can be submitted on the strength of a valid explicit witness.
